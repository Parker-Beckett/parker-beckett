Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Movies”

The Guy Across the Table

My wife hates this song.

I was thinking about some of the people I deceived in my past. Not necessarily by telling them things that weren't true, but by showing them a version of myself that left out some pretty important information.

When I was living in Fort Smith, Arkansas, there was an Italian restaurant that didn't have a liquor license. That made it a pretty good place for me to take a date.

For one, I didn't have to pay for anybody's drinks. And two, I didn't have to do my little routine where I'd order one beer, pretend that was enough, and call it a night.

Because when I started drinking, I liked to keep going.

I didn't want to drink and drive, either. I'd rather wait until I got home, where I could drink the way I wanted to.

So there I'd be, sitting across the table from somebody, putting on this version of myself. The radio guy. Funny. Got dimples. Could make you laugh. Probably seemed like a pretty decent guy.

And those things weren't necessarily fake. But they sure weren't the whole story.

Because underneath all that, I was a raging alcoholic.

I wasn't going on these dates trying to take somebody home to have sex. That wasn't what I was after. But I was putting on a front, and the person sitting across from me wasn't getting anything close to the complete picture.

Looking back, that's sad to me.

At the time, I don't think I fully understood what I was doing. I knew I had a problem with alcohol, but I didn't necessarily recognize how much of my life was organized around keeping it out of sight.

Even choosing a restaurant had something to do with drinking.

When I started writing "Movies," I was thinking about those people and the impressions I'd left them with. The difference between the guy they thought they were meeting and the guy who couldn't wait to get home.

There was another part of writing this song that I enjoyed, though.

I've always liked Led Zeppelin's "Fool in the Rain." There's a particular moment in that song that I've always heard as a little cocky. That's just my interpretation, but I love the attitude of it. Almost like somebody saying, Yeah, right. You think I'm sitting here feeling blue?

I wanted to see if I could work a little tribute to that into something I wrote. Partly because I liked it, and partly because I was testing myself as a songwriter.

So I gave it a shot.

That's really where "Movies" came from. Looking back at who I used to be, thinking about the people who only saw what I wanted them to see, and finding a way to put some of that into a song.

I could be pretty convincing sitting across that table.

The problem was, I knew who was going home afterward.
