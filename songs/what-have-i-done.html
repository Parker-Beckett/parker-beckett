Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “What Have I Done?”

The Moment It Hit Me

It was January 2015 when my coworker Steve told me I looked jaundiced.

I didn't really know what that meant.

So I got on the computer and looked it up.

Liver failure.

Whoa.

I remember looking in the mirror after reading that and thinking, yeah, I guess I am pretty yellow.

I'd been having stomach pain for weeks. Steve had been telling me I needed to go to the doctor. He was pretty persistent about it, and eventually I did.

But that moment at the computer is something I'll never forget.

Someone had asked me what it felt like when I was going into the hospital. What was going through my head? What did that moment feel like?

And when I started thinking about writing "What Have I Done?" my mind went straight back to that day.

Because I remember the feeling.

What have I done?

It was overwhelming. Almost like everything I'd been told about my drinking suddenly came crashing down on me.

People had been telling me to stop for years. Stop drinking. Stop drinking.

But I didn't feel like anybody was showing me how.

And at that point in my life, I thought I needed alcohol. My body told me I needed it. My brain told me I needed it.

Trying to explain that to somebody who hasn't experienced it is difficult. It would have been like somebody telling me I couldn't eat anymore. That's how necessary alcohol felt to me.

And now I was sitting there reading about what it had done to my body.

I remember thinking, You did it this time, man. You really messed up.

I eventually saw Dr. David Cross, a GI doctor in Fort Smith, Arkansas, and ended up at Mercy Hospital.

Dr. Cross was amazing. So were the people who took care of me. I'm incredibly grateful to them because they saved my life.

I was told that 98 percent of people in my condition don't survive.

So I guess that makes me a two-percenter.

I look at that as a miracle. I believe God saved my life for a reason.

But "What Have I Done?" isn't really about what happened afterward. It's about that first moment when I began to understand just how serious things had become.

Before the doctors. Before the hospital. Before I knew whether I was going to survive.

Just me, standing there after looking something up on the internet, trying to make sense of what I'd read.

And realizing that everybody who'd been worried about me might have been right.

What have I done?
