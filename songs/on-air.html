Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “On Air”

The Man Behind the Microphone

When people asked me what I wanted to do when I grew up, I always had an answer.

I wanted to entertain people.

I was seventeen when I got hired at a radio station. I started as a board operator and worked my way up. Overnights, weekend nights, nights, middays, afternoons. There was always another shift to work, another opportunity to chase.

And I loved the idea of being on the radio.

Being a local celebrity was pretty cool, too. I wasn't making much money, but people knew who I was. That felt like something when I was young.

For a while, I kept chasing the next thing. A better shift, another station, a bigger market. Eventually, I made it to Dallas.

And I remember thinking, this isn't really any better.

Somewhere along the way, I'd gotten comfortable in radio. I knew how to do it. I knew how to be that guy.

And I think that became part of the problem.

I remember somebody in my family, maybe my mom or grandma, telling me they always knew they could turn on the radio, hear me, and know I was doing okay.

But I wasn't okay.

Maybe that's how it sounded coming through the speakers. I could get on the air, entertain people, and make it seem like everything was going great.

The thing is, I didn't even recognize what was happening with my mental health at the time. I wasn't happy, but I didn't really understand why. Alcohol had become my Band-Aid. It was how I dealt with things I didn't know how to deal with, or maybe didn't even recognize yet.

And from the outside, I was doing what I'd always said I wanted to do.

Living the dream, right?

Except it didn't feel like one.

When everybody knows you as that guy on the radio, it can feel like you're supposed to have everything together. I remember feeling like I couldn't even ask for help because I was supposed to be somebody who was doing fine.

I've spent a lot of time thinking about how my addiction to alcohol kept me stuck for so many years. But looking at the things I'm doing now, I started wondering how much radio kept me stuck, too.

I learned a lot, and I don't regret those years. I got to entertain people, which was exactly what I'd wanted.

But I wonder how long I stayed because it was familiar. Because I'd built an identity around it. Because I knew how to do radio, even when I wasn't happy doing it anymore.

The industry has changed so much since I started. The opportunities to learn from somebody, work your way up, and become the next person on the air don't seem to be there the way they used to be. That's sad to me.

But there are also more ways to entertain people now than there ever were.

I'm writing music. I'm making films. I'm doing things I probably wouldn't have imagined doing when I was seventeen.

And that's what got me thinking about "On Air."

All those years, I thought radio was my dream.

But when I really think about what I used to say back in high school, I never said I wanted to spend my whole life in radio.

I said I wanted to entertain people.

I guess I just thought the microphone was the only way to do it.
