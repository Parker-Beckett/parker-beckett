Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Fort Smith”

Eight Years in Fort Smith

I spent eight years living in Fort Smith, Arkansas, and I almost didn't make it out alive.

That's not really an indictment of Fort Smith. It's just where I happened to be during some of the hardest years of my life.

I was lonely. I was lost. I didn't understand much about life, addiction, or even myself. Looking back, I sometimes think of it as being alone in the wilderness. I was trying to find my way through something I didn't really understand, and there were times when things got pretty bad.

Somehow, I survived it.

But that's not actually what I was thinking about when I started writing this song.

I was thinking about a friend of mine who owns a bar down there. She's doing well, and I was happy to hear that. Then I started thinking about another good friend who's in recovery. I love hearing from her when she checks in. Every now and then, I send her some Parker Beckett music. She's in recovery, so she's one of our people.

And before long, I was thinking about all the people I knew in Fort Smith.

Man, I met some good people there.

Some I'm still in touch with. Some aren't with us anymore. And some I haven't seen or talked to in years. But I remember them.

Now, I should probably mention that Fort Smith is also one of the strangest places I've ever lived.

I've said it a million times: Stephen King couldn't write some of the shit that happened in that town.

I remember hearing about a guy who got caught doing something incredibly inappropriate with a pole outside a school in the middle of the night. Apparently, the police knew exactly who it was because he'd done it something like seven times before.

Seven times.

I remember thinking, how the hell does somebody get to seven?

And that was just one story. Every week, it seemed like something else happened that made you wonder how anybody could possibly come up with this stuff.

I've actually got a movie planned about Fort Smith. Believe me, there's material.

But here's the thing.

As crazy as some of those years were, when I look back now, I don't think about Fort Smith as a bad place. I think about the people who were good to me when I wasn't doing particularly well.

People who knew I was struggling and still came around.

I sometimes wish I could go back there at the age I am now, with a little more maturity and a better understanding of life. I think I'd appreciate those people differently. Maybe I'd appreciate the place differently, too.

For a long time, I thought the town was crazy and everybody around me was crazy. It took leaving Arkansas and getting some distance from that part of my life to recognize how much of the trouble I was carrying around myself.

That's why the chorus says, “If I survived Fort Smith, I can make it anywhere.”

It's not about surviving Fort Smith itself. It's about surviving who I was and what I was going through while I lived there.

And I don't think I would have made it through those years without some of the people I met.

That's really why I wanted to write this song.

I wanted my friends down there to know that I still think about them. That I remember how good they were to me, even when I probably wasn't the easiest person to be around.

Fort Smith was eight years of my life. Some of the hardest years.

But there were good people in those years.

And I'll never forget them.
