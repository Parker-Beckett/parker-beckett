Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Run It Straight”

A Different Man Came Home

I never knew my grandfather. And for most of my life, I didn't know much about the Winfield side of my family, either.

When I started writing "Run It Straight," I'd been thinking about death quite a bit. My family, my past, the people who came before me. After learning about my ADHD symptoms, I'd started wondering where some of those traits might have come from. Was it my mom's side? My dad's?

That curiosity got me digging into my family history.

And man, some of what I learned was heartbreaking.

My grandfather was a World War II veteran. He'd been shot and wounded during the war. From what I learned, he came home something of a hero. He didn't have to work for a year.

I imagine there were plenty of people willing to buy him a drink, too. I don't know that for certain, but I can picture what that kind of homecoming might have been like.

And alcohol eventually chased him away from everybody.

I'd heard stories about him over the years, and they weren't particularly good ones. I never knew the man myself, so those stories were really all I had.

But there was something else my dad had passed along.

According to the stories he'd heard, my grandfather had been a much different man before the war. A better man, from the way people remembered him. The man who came home wasn't the same person.

My dad wasn't even born when his father went off to war. He hadn't witnessed that change himself. These were stories that had been passed down through the family.

And when I started thinking about that, I thought, Oh my God. Of course he was different.

He'd been overseas fighting in a war. He'd been wounded. I have no idea what he saw or what he experienced, or what it was like for him to come home and try to return to a normal life.

I can't help wondering how many other men came home carrying things their families never fully understood.

And the more I thought about my grandfather, the more it broke my heart.

Because I know something about what alcohol can do to a person. I almost died from it myself. I know how horrible that can be.

Thinking about him going through something like that, especially after everything he'd already survived, was crushing.

I wish I'd known him. I wish I could have heard his side of the story instead of only knowing the pieces that were left behind.

That's where "Run It Straight" came from. I started out wondering about my own brain and my family history, and ended up thinking about a man whose life I suddenly felt much closer to.

I don't know everything that happened to my grandfather. I probably never will.

All I have are the stories that were passed down about him. Stories about a man who went away to war and came home different.

And I wish I'd had the chance to know who he was before.
