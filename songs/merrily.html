Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Merrily”

A Different Kind of Merrily

I've heard "Row, Row, Row Your Boat" about a billion times in my life.

Now that I'm a dad, I'm hearing it a billion more.

I love fatherhood.

I was sitting with Parker, watching Ms. Rachel, and she was singing that song. Something I'd heard since I was a kid, probably without giving the words much thought.

But this time, something about it got me thinking.

Life is but a dream.

I started thinking about my own life, the dreams I'd had, where I thought I'd be, and where I actually was. Some things had turned out differently than I imagined. Other things I was right in the middle of experiencing.

And I had some feelings about all of that.

That's really where the idea started. Just sitting there with Parker, listening to a children's song and thinking about how differently those words sounded to me now.

Then I started playing around with the writing.

I happen to know a woman named Merrily. That's exactly how she spells her name, just like the word in the children's song.

And I started thinking, what if I could write something that sounded like I was talking to somebody named Merrily, while also playing with the original meaning of the word?

I thought that could be fun.

So I started writing something that was almost like a response to Ms. Rachel and that little song I'd been hearing over and over.

I've had people tell me "Merrily" doesn't make sense.

Maybe that's partly because I'm playing with those two meanings. I don't know. Sometimes an idea makes perfect sense in your head, and then you try explaining it to somebody else.

But I've listened to "Don't Stop Believin'" by Journey my whole life, and there's no South Detroit. There's a river there.

So I figure I'm in pretty good company.

Anyway, that's where "Merrily" came from.

Parker was watching Ms. Rachel.

I was sitting there thinking about my life and seeing if I could turn a children's song into something of my own.
