Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Sorry”

When Sorry Means Something

Somewhere along the line, I got pretty good at saying sorry.

Whether I actually meant it was another story.

I think it was the last time I was in treatment when I met a therapist named Stephen. He was also a person in recovery, and he ran some of the groups I attended.

Stephen was an interesting guy because, if I'd seen him in a store or a restaurant back then, I probably would've decided I didn't like him before he even opened his mouth.

He looked like somebody who was doing better than me.

And at that point in my life, that was apparently enough of a reason not to like somebody.

But in those groups, I loved the guy.

He was willing to be vulnerable. He'd open up about his own life, and somehow that made it okay for the rest of us to open up, too. I was fortunate to end up in his groups. They were some of the best I ever attended.

One of the things Stephen talked about was saying sorry.

I started thinking about how I'd used those words throughout my life. Especially with my mom.

I'd say, "Sorry, Mom," almost like that was supposed to take care of everything. I'd said the words, so maybe she'd forgive me, and we could move on.

But when I really started looking back, I realized there were plenty of times I wasn't sorry at all.

I just wanted forgiveness. Or I wanted whatever I'd done to stop being a problem.

That's a pretty big difference.

Stephen got me thinking about why I was apologizing in the first place. Was I saying it because I understood what I'd done and genuinely regretted it? Or because I wanted something from the person I was apologizing to?

That conversation stayed with me.

These days, I'm much more selective about saying sorry. Not because I think there's anything wrong with apologizing, but because I want those words to mean something when they come out of my mouth.

There's a difference between telling somebody you're sorry something happened to them and apologizing for something you actually did.

I think about that now.

I don't find myself having to apologize nearly as often as I used to. But I still make mistakes. Of course I do. And when I do, I want to be able to say I'm sorry and actually mean it.

That's where "Sorry" came from.

Looking back at all the times I'd said those words without really understanding why, and thinking about the kind of person I want to be when I say them now.

I still say sorry.

I just want it to mean something.
