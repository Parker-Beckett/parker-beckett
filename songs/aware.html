Story Behind the Song

The Story Behind “Aware”

Don't Let Them Take It From You

It was three or four in the morning, and I was awake again.

I have nights like that. Can't sleep, so I end up on my phone, looking at something on Facebook. And before I know it, I'm going down another rabbit hole.

Man, there's so much garbage out there.

I don't know how else to say it. So much of what you see seems designed to irritate you. It's like somebody's constantly poking at you, trying to get a reaction. Even the things you enjoy somehow become something people want to argue about or ruin for everybody else.

And it's exhausting.

I've gotten to where I have a hard time even watching television. There's so much on there that I just don't want to see. I don't enjoy it. I don't want to spend my time being irritated by things I never asked to be part of in the first place.

But there I was, at three or four in the morning, scrolling through more of it.

That's what got me thinking about "Aware."

Because I can see what's happening. I'm aware of how these things affect me. I recognize when something is getting under my skin or making me feel worse.

And sometimes I keep looking anyway.

But here's what I started thinking about.

I can only control what I can control.

I can't control what people post online. I can't control what somebody puts on television or what everybody decides to be upset about tomorrow. And I certainly can't fix all of it.

But I don't have to live in that world twenty-four hours a day, either.

I don't have to give it all my attention.

There comes a point where you have to know your limits. You have to recognize what you can take and when you've had enough. And sometimes protecting yourself is as simple as turning the damn thing off.

I think there's more power in that than we realize.

Because what you let into your head matters. What you spend your time thinking about matters. And if you constantly let other people decide what you're supposed to be angry about, pretty soon they're taking up space in your life that you could've spent on something you actually enjoy.

That's really where "Aware" came from.

I'm not pretending I've figured it all out. Obviously, if I'm sitting there scrolling through Facebook at four in the morning, I've still got some work to do.

But I'm aware of it.

And I think that's a good place to start.

You can't control everything that's happening around you.

But you can control how much of it you let take away from your own life.

Don't let them take that from you.
